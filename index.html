01 — The idea

What is a keiretsu?

A keiretsu (系列) is a Japanese business network. After the war, independent firms tied themselves to each other — shared banks, crossed shareholdings, and a standing meeting that ran on the same day every month for decades.

They gave up some speed for something you can't buy: people who pick up the phone, and who are still there in a bad year. The real thing had real flaws. The instinct still travels, and it's the one this site is built on.

What we borrow from it

Show up for each other
Nobody keeps a ledger. Everybody keeps count.
Keep the rhythm
It happens whether or not anyone feels like it.
Stay small
Four to eight people. Any more and it stops being a circle.
Think in years
The compounding is the point.

What a mastermind is

Peers who meet on a fixed schedule to work on each other's real problems. One person takes the hot seat. The rest ask questions before they give advice.

What kills them

Not the idea — the reply-all thread about Tuesdays. Settle the hour once and the circle lives long enough to be worth something.

What good looks like

Five people, ninety minutes, same slot every fortnight. And a rule that you say the thing you'd rather not say out loud.

03 — In the room

What people actually ask

Stuck on what to bring, or what to say when it's someone else's turn? Start here.

Take to the hot seat

  • Here's the decision I keep putting off.
  • I'm about to spend real money on this. Talk me out of it.
  • This number is going the wrong way and I don't know why.
  • I said I'd do something last time. I didn't. Here's why.
  • I need to let someone go and I've been avoiding it.
  • What would I do if I weren't scared?

Ask before you advise

  • What have you already tried?
  • What would have to be true for this to work?
  • What are you actually afraid of here?
  • If nothing changes, where are you in six months?
  • Is this your problem, or someone else's?
  • What's the smallest version you could test this week?

Then close the same way every time: what are you doing before we next meet? Next session starts by asking whether you did it.

08 — FAQ

Questions about the tool

Do I need an account?

No. There's nothing to sign up for and nothing to log into.

Where does my data live?

In this browser, on this device. It isn't sent anywhere — there's no server behind this page. Clear it any time with Reset all data at the bottom.

How do people join my circle?

Each person fills in the form and paints their week, then sends you their link. You paste it into Add a member and they appear in your circle.

Someone's availability changed. Now what?

They repaint their week and send a fresh link. Paste it in and it replaces what you had for them. Links are matched on email, so use the same one.

Does it really handle timezones?

Yes. Everyone paints in their own local time and the overlap is worked out across all of them, half-hour offsets and midnight wraps included. Each suggested window shows what time it lands for everybody.

Will the invite actually repeat?

Yes — it's a standard recurring event. Weekly, fortnightly, or the same weekday every month. Google Calendar, Outlook, Apple Calendar and Fastmail all understand it.

What if no slot works for everyone?

You still get the best windows, and each one names who'd miss it. Usually the answer is a shorter session, not a different week.

How do I move the meeting later on?

Pick a new block and download again. Be warned: it arrives as a separate series rather than an edit of the old one, so delete the previous event first.
